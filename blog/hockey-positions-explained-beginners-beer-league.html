Hockey Positions Explained for Beginners (Beer League Edition)

October 12, 2026 • 7 min read • By Utah Glizzies HC

You showed up to your first beer league game, someone said "you're on left wing," and you nodded like you knew what that meant. We've all been there. Here's what every hockey position actually does, written for adults who started skating after they started paying a mortgage.

The Basics: Five Skaters and One Goalie

Each team has six players on the ice: three forwards (a center and two wingers), two defensemen, and a goaltender. Forwards focus on scoring, defensemen protect the zone and start breakouts, and the goalie is the last line of defense (and the only person allowed to treat the crease like a personal apartment).

Center: The Cardio Position

The center takes faceoffs, plays both ends of the ice, and connects everyone. In beer league that means a lot of skating. If your lungs are great and your chirps are better, center is your spot. Nobody said it was fun after the third shift, though.

Wingers: Shoot First, Back-Check Eventually

Left wing and right wing patrol the boards, finish plays, and get open for passes. Wing is the friendliest spot for new players because the job is simple: stay on your side, shoot when you can, and help in the defensive zone. Pro tip: that last part is not optional.

Defense: Part Bodyguard, Part Quarterback

Defensemen clear the front of the net, break up plays, and make the first pass out. A good beer league D-man keeps it simple: don't get beat wide, move the puck, and don't try the toe drag. If you can skate backwards even a little, this spot is for you. If you can't yet, our guide on how to stop in hockey is a good first step.

The best beer league defenseman is the one you forget about because nothing bad happened.

Goalie: The Most Wanted Person in the Rink

Every rec league needs goalies, and almost nobody wants to be one. It takes serious gear and a certain mental toughness, but a willing goalie never has to look for a team. Just know that the gear is its own budget conversation. Start with our equipment checklist to see what you'd be signing up for.

Which Position Should You Pick?

New to the game? Try wing or defense, tell your captain your skill level honestly (see beer league skill levels explained), and rotate as you get comfortable.
