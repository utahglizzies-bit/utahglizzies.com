Do You Need a Cage in Beer League Hockey? Cage vs. Visor

October 6, 2026 • 6 min read • By Utah Glizzies HC

Every beer league locker room has one: the guy in a visor who skates like he's auditioning for the pros, and the guy in a full cage who is, frankly, the smartest person in the room. So which one are you? Here's our honest, chirp-free take on cage vs. visor in adult rec hockey.

The Short Answer

Check your league rules first. Then, if you can choose, a full cage is the safest default for most beer leaguers, especially new ones. A full cage protects the whole face. A visor gives you clearer sightlines and airflow but leaves your nose, mouth and jaw out there on their own.

Cage vs. Visor at a Glance

  • Full cage: The most complete facial coverage, a good fit for beginners and anyone who'd like to keep their teeth. Trade-offs: slightly more restricted view and some fogging.
  • Half visor: Better vision and airflow, but a visor does not provide the same full-face coverage as a cage. Pair it with a mouthguard if your league allows it.
Your face has no backup copy. Your pride does.

"But the NHL Guys Wear Visors"

They do, and they've also been skating since they could walk, play against opponents who can control a shot, and have a team dentist on speed dial. Recreational players are usually better served by comfort, reliable protection and fit than by copying elite players. Beer league pucks come from a lot of directions, and not all of them are aimed.

How to Choose

  1. Read your league's rules. Some require facial protection, some only recommend it. Rules beat opinions, including ours.
  2. Be honest about your skill level. New players fall, get tangled, and take stray sticks. Full cage until you're comfortable.
  3. Get the fit right. A helmet that sits properly and stays put matters more than the logo. Try it on before you buy.
  4. Add a mouthguard. Cheap, unglamorous, and very good at keeping your smile.
