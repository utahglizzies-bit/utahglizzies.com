Stick and Puck in Utah: Where to Practice Hockey Between Games

October 6, 2026 • 6 min read • By Utah Glizzies HC

You signed up for a beer league, your first game is in two weeks, and you just realized you've never actually shot a puck at a net that wasn't a driveway goal. Welcome to the club. The fix is cheap, low-pressure, and sitting on the Wasatch Front right now: stick and puck.

What Is Stick and Puck?

Stick and puck is open ice for hockey players. No game, no coach, no referee, no one yelling "your other left." You show up with a stick, a puck, and your gear, and you skate, shoot, and stickhandle at your own pace. It's the beer leaguer's gym, and it is where most of us got from "falling over" to "falling over while holding a stick."

Where to Find Stick and Puck and Open Skate in Utah

Schedules and prices change, so treat this as a map, not a contract, and confirm with the rink before you drive over.

  • Salt Lake County Ice Center (Murray): Located at 5201 S. Murray Park Lane, it has listed stick-and-puck sessions at roughly $7 a skater, with registration online at slco.org/county-ice. This is the first place we'd look.
  • Utah Mammoth Ice Center (Sandy): Our home rink lists public open skate Friday through Sunday at about $12. It's regular skating rather than a shooting session, but it's great for building edges and confidence. More in our Utah Mammoth Ice Center guide.
  • Gallivan Center (downtown SLC): A seasonal outdoor rink with open skate and no reservations needed. Not a stick-and-puck spot, but a fun way to log ice hours when it opens for winter.

Want the full rink list? Our directory of ice rinks in Utah covers the rest.

What to Bring

Rinks typically require a helmet at minimum and recommend gloves and shin protection. If you're borrowing or renting, ask the rink what they offer. If you're buying, our beginner equipment checklist and our guide to where to buy and rent hockey gear in Utah will keep you from overspending.

A 30-Minute Stick-and-Puck Plan for Beginners

  1. 5 minutes: skate laps. Forward, backward, tight turns. Get the legs warm.
  2. 5 minutes: stops. Practice both directions. If stopping is your weak spot, read our beginner guide to stopping.
  3. 10 minutes: stickhandling. Puck out in front, head up, slow and clean beats fast and sloppy.
  4. 10 minutes: shooting. Wrist shots first. Aim for the corners, then try to hit the net at all, which is a perfectly respectable goal.
Ten reps with your head up beats a hundred reps staring at the puck.

Stick-and-Puck Etiquette

Keep your head up, call out when you're cutting across, don't blast pucks at people, and give beginners space. Everyone there is working on something. Most of us are working on everything.
