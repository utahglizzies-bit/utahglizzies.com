Where to Buy & Rent Hockey Gear in Utah

September 7, 2026 • 8 min read • By Utah Glizzies HC

The Utah Mammoth turned half the state into hockey people overnight, and now a very specific problem has followed: a lot of you want to actually play, and nobody told you where to buy the stuff. Sticks, skates, a bag full of pads that smells like regret — where does a brand-new Utah player even get it? Good news. Gearing up here is easier than it was a couple years ago, and you do not have to drive to Colorado to do it. Here's the local Glizzy-approved map.

The One Dedicated Hockey Pro Shop: Pure Hockey (Murray)

If you only remember one name, remember this one. Pure Hockey in Murray is the closest thing the Salt Lake Valley has to a real, walk-in hockey pro shop — a full floor of skates, sticks, protective gear, and a dedicated goalie department for the brave souls among us. This is where you go when you want to actually hold three sticks, flex them, and feel which one is right instead of guessing off a website.

Why a pro shop matters for a beginner: skate fitting. Skates are the one piece of gear you should never buy blind. A shop can bake and heat-mold your boot to your foot, which is the difference between "hockey is fun" and "why are my feet on fire after one shift." They'll also sharpen on-site.

Buy your skates in person. Everything else you can shop around for. Feet do not forgive.

Big-Box Backup: Scheels

Not ready to walk into a specialty shop? Scheels carries a legitimately good hockey section for a general sporting-goods store — sticks, entry-level protective gear, tape, pucks, and accessories — and the stores are famously well-organized. It's a great spot to grab the small stuff (tape, laces, a water bottle, shin guards) and a solid fallback if you want to see gear in person on a weekend without a special trip.

Gearing Up Without Going Broke: Used & Starter Gear

Here's the secret every beer leaguer knows: you do not need everything new. Protective gear — shoulder pads, elbow pads, shin guards, pants, gloves — takes abuse and then sits in a bag. Buying it lightly used is completely normal and saves a fortune.

  • Play It Again Sports — used and clearance gear, perfect for a first kit you're not sure you'll keep forever.
  • Facebook Marketplace & local hockey groups — Utah's hockey community is growing fast, and people are constantly offloading full bags of gently-used gear. Search "hockey gear" in the Salt Lake area and you'll find full sets cheap.
  • End-of-season clearance — pro shops and big-box stores mark down last year's models in spring. Same gear, smaller price.

The rule of thumb: buy skates and a helmet with care, buy the padding cheap. Your helmet is the one used-gear exception — if you go used, make sure it's not expired and hasn't taken a big hit. For a full breakdown of what a first kit even includes, we wrote a beer league equipment checklist for beginners that covers every piece so you don't show up missing something dumb.

Try Before You Buy: Rink Rentals & Loaner Gear

The smartest move a brand-new player can make is to not buy anything at all — at first. Adult Learn to Play programs at Utah rinks, including the Utah Mammoth Ice Center in Sandy, typically include loaner or rental gear as part of the program. You get on the ice, figure out if you actually love it (you will), and only then drop money on your own kit.

This is genuinely the path we recommend: rent through a learn-to-play session, borrow a stick, skate a few times, and let the sport sell itself before your credit card commits. Our full guide on how to start playing hockey as an adult in Utah walks through those programs step by step.

Don't Forget: Skate Sharpening

New players ask this constantly, so here's the answer: get your skates sharpened, and do it regularly. Pure Hockey sharpens in-store, most rink pro shops offer it, and there are independent sharpeners around the valley. A standard hollow (ask for a 1/2" if you have no idea) is fine to start. Re-sharpen every 8 to 12 hours of ice time, or sooner if you're slipping on your edges. Dull skates make you feel like you forgot how to skate — it's not you, it's the blade.

What It All Costs

Ballpark, so you're not blindsided at the register:

$400+
Full head-to-toe entry-level kit, bought smart with some used gear
$700
Roughly the top of the range for all-new beginner gear
$8–12
Typical skate sharpening, every 8–12 hours of ice

The Short Version

Buy skates in person at Pure Hockey in Murray and get them fitted. Grab the small stuff at Scheels. Save money on protective gear via Play It Again Sports and Marketplace. And before you spend a dime, rent through a rink's learn-to-play program to make sure you love it. Utah's a real hockey state now. Gearing up is the easy part — we'll handle the rest.
