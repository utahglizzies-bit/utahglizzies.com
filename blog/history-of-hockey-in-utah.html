History of Hockey in Utah: From the Golden Eagles to the Glizzies

August 3, 2026 • 8 min read • By Utah Glizzies HC

Everyone assumes hockey showed up in Utah when the NHL did. It didn't. This state has been fielding professional hockey teams since 1969 — through three different franchises, three different leagues, a record-setting championship crowd, and a decades-long gap with no pro team at all — before the Utah Mammoth ever dropped a puck. Here's the full arc, and where a beer-league team named after hot dogs fits into it.

The Original Pros: The Salt Lake Golden Eagles (1969–1994)

Pro hockey in Utah started with the Salt Lake Golden Eagles, who played their first game in 1969 at the old Salt Palace and later at the Maverik Center. For 25 seasons, the Golden Eagles were Utah's only professional sports team, bouncing through three different leagues — the Western Hockey League, the Central Hockey League, and the International Hockey League — as minor pro hockey reorganized around them.

The Golden Eagles weren't just around, they were good. The franchise picked up three Adams Cup championships in the CHL and two Turner Cup championships in the IHL before finally being sold and relocated to Auburn Hills, Michigan in 1994.

The Grizzlies Era and a Record-Setting Crowd

Utah went one season without pro hockey after the Golden Eagles left, then got a new team almost immediately: the reigning Turner Cup champion Denver Grizzlies relocated to the Delta Center for the 1995–96 season as the Utah Grizzlies. They didn't miss a beat — the Grizzlies won a second consecutive Turner Cup that same year.

The clinching game is the detail worth remembering: 17,381 fans packed the Delta Center to watch it, which was, at the time, the largest single-game attendance figure in minor-league hockey history. Three decades before an NHL sellout streak, Utah had already proven it would show up for hockey in force.

Thirty years before the Mammoth sold out an NHL arena, a minor-league Utah team sold out the same building for a championship — and set a record doing it.

The Long Gap Before the NHL

Pro hockey eventually left Utah again, and the state went years without a top-tier team while the sport kept a quieter, steadier home base: local rinks, youth programs, and the adult rec leagues that have run continuously through every era of Utah hockey — Golden Eagles, Grizzlies, and the years in between. Long before "Utah hockey market" was a phrase anyone used, there were beer leaguers at rinks like the Utah Olympic Oval and Cottonwood Heights Ice Arena keeping the game alive with zero fanfare.

The NHL Arrives: From Utah Hockey Club to the Utah Mammoth

The NHL officially landed in Utah in April 2024, when the league's board of governors unanimously approved a $1.2 billion sale that relocated the Arizona Coyotes to Salt Lake City. The team played its first season as the placeholder-named Utah Hockey Club, then became the Utah Mammoth for the 2025–26 season. We covered the full impact of that arrival — sellouts, ticket demand, the first playoff berth — in how the Utah Mammoth changed Utah hockey.

The Mammoth's momentum hasn't slowed down. The 2026–27 season brings an 84-game schedule (Utah's first in 30-plus years) and the franchise is set to host the NHL Winter Classic at Rice-Eccles Stadium on December 31, 2026 — another marker of just how fast this market has grown.

Where Beer League Fits Into the Story

Here's the part most history write-ups skip: Utah hockey has never really been about one franchise. Golden Eagles fans became Grizzlies fans became rec-league players became Mammoth season-ticket holders — the roster of teams changed, but the people playing pickup and beer league in Sandy, Cottonwood Heights, and Salt Lake City kept the sport running through every gap. The Utah Glizzies are the latest link in that chain: a beer league team built for the wave of new players the Mammoth pulled off the couch and onto the ice.
